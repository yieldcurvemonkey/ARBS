1. BAR-INDEXING RULE + a worked example
==============================================================================
price(T) = Close of the LAST bar labelled STRICTLY BEFORE T (bars are start-stamped, so that close is a print at or before T); NaN if staleness = T-(L+1min) exceeds 15 min. Baseline for the cumulative path = offset -60 min.

Why not the close of the bar labelled T: Barchart bars are START-stamped, so
the bar labelled 10:00 covers [10:00, 10:01) and its close is a print from
AFTER 10:00. Using it for 'the price at 10:00' puts the speech's own reaction
into the pre-event baseline and manufactures a step at offset 0.

print that had already happened at T). The rule is identical at every offset,
so it cannot create a step at 0 - it can only understate the reaction.

WHAT THE TWO EXTREMES ACTUALLY ARE - and the limitation they expose.
Both largest moves are macro events, not the speech:
  * the hawk, 2025-04-09, is the 90-day tariff-pause announcement (~13:18 ET).
    The path is flat through +120 and then moves 29bp between +120 and +180 -
    two hours after an 11:00 speech. NO calendar column in this panel flags it.
  * the dove, 2023-11-14, is the CPI downside surprise at 08:30 ET, five
    offsets before a 05:30 speech's +240. is_cpi_day DOES flag that one.
The +240/+300 window is five hours wide and the front end is not quiet for
five hours. This panel flags CPI, NFP and FOMC decision days and NOTHING ELSE,
so tariff headlines, Treasury refundings, geopolitics and ECB/BoE spillover sit
inside these windows unlabelled. That is a property of the question, not a bug
in the build - but a far-offset result must be read as 'what happened in the
five hours after a speech', not 'what the speech did'. The placebo band is the
only defence, and it is time-of-day, weekday and calendar-proximity matched
precisely so that ambient five-hour drift is inside the null rather than the
signal.

STANCE_SIGN vs BUCKET - they are NOT the same cut, and the difference is big.
stance_sign is sign(stance_score_exante). `bucket` is the repo's own
absolute_bucket(), which calls |score| < 10 NEUTRAL (0). So an event can be
signed dove and bucket-neutral - the exemplar dove above, Jefferson at -5.0,
is exactly that. Cross-count of signed events:
  bucket       -2  -1    0    1    2
  stance_sign                       
  -1           87  23  108    0    0
   1            0   0  261  332  174
  369 of 985 signed events (37%) sit in the |score| < 10 band the repo calls neutral.
  So n_hawk=767 / n_dove=218 OVERSTATE conviction if read against
  the repo's convention. Filtering |bucket| >= 1 leaves 616 events (hawk 506, dove 110).
  Both columns are stored; the headline chart has to pick one and say which.
