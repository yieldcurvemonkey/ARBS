The cross-sectional standard deviation of a bond's richness against its local fitted curve is the entire dispersion a butterfly among near-identical maturities can capture; the dashed lines are each fund's own measured round trip on FedInvest's published bid and offer. Medians, not means, because the pack's 0.81x headline is a median and the mean is pulled up by a handful of dislocated days.
LEFT: the dispersion is FLAT around the reconstitution -- 11% from the narrowest cell to the widest in TLT and 8% in TLH, with no step at day 0. The day on which the twelve funds do 17% of their measured trading, and TLT 7% of its own (figure 3), is not a day on which the bonds they trade are dislocated relative to each other. That is the single most useful negative in this pack: it says the reconstitution is absorbed, and it says so without reference to any signal.
RIGHT: there IS monthly variation -- 29% peak-to-trough in TLT, 28% in TLH -- so the ceiling is genuinely seasonal. It does not help. TLT's widest month, Sep, reaches 0.483bp, which is 0.90x of one round trip against 0.81x pooled: 0 of 12 months put a full round trip of dispersion on the table, and a signal would have to explain more than all of it. TLH is the one place the lines cross -- its dispersion clears its own 1.00bp round trip in 5 months, by at most 1.06x. A 6% margin over gross cost, before any signal is asked to explain any of it, is not an opening; it is stated because the panel shows the crossing and a caption that did not mention it would be reading the figure for the reader.
The two funds also disagree about WHICH month (profile correlation -0.16, peaks in Sep and Nov), so even the ceiling's seasonality is not something to schedule around. No Newey-West verdict is quoted here because a dispersion level is not a return; the claim is a comparison of two measured levels, and both are below the line.
