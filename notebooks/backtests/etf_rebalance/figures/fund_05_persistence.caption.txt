Persistence of TLT's active weights. LEFT: the three most overweight and three most underweight names in TLT's book on 2023-06-01, followed forward for 252 business days. The selection rule is stated so it can be checked - rank by active weight on the anchor date, take the top three and bottom three, and never revisit; nothing to the right of the vertical line is used to choose the names. The grey band is the inter-quartile range of the whole board on each day, so the extremes can be read against the day's dispersion. RIGHT: the same question over the whole sample, against the control. WHAT IT PROVES. The gaps are not dislocations. A name picked as the most overweight in the fund is still the most overweight name 86% of the time three weeks later and 50% of the time a YEAR later, against a reshuffling baseline of about 3%. Neighbouring bonds run hundreds of bp of active weight in opposite directions and simply stay there. As a description of how a sampling fund holds a curve this is real, measured, and the most robust thing in the pack. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE. Anything tradeable - and the right-hand panel is why. The richest bond is still the richest bond only 59% of the time three weeks out and 20% a year out; the richness residual's own OU half-life is 17 business days. So the holdings signal is very nearly a constant and the thing it would have to forecast turns over roughly monthly. A constant cannot forecast that, and the measurement agrees: active weight's 63-day IC is -0.065 (backwards) raw and +0.005bp per unit z with Newey-West t = 0.89 once richness is controlled for, against a 0.535bp round trip. Persistence is exactly what makes this a portrait rather than a signal - a gap that never closes is not an opportunity, it is a policy.
