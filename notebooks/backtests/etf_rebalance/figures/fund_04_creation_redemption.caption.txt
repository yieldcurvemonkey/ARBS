TLT's shares outstanding, 2016-2026, and what a creation basket contains. 278 of 2,520 usable document pairs moved the share count by 2% or more (154 creations, 124 redemptions). WHAT IT PROVES. Creation and redemption is not an occasional event - TLT went from 51m to 565m shares in hundreds of discrete steps, the largest a single-day 14.0%. But the two lower panels are the finding, and it is a negative: a basket is overwhelmingly the fund made bigger rather than a set of bonds anyone picked. One pro-rata scaling of the whole book explains a median 99.9% of the gross par movement on a flow day, and on the largest creation in the sample (2019-04-18, +14.0%) 30 of 34 bonds sit within half a percentage point of the same scaling factor. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE. That there is no discretion at all - the distribution has a real left tail, with a 5th percentile of 61% on flow days. But note which way the comparison runs: an ordinary day is LESS pro-rata than a creation day (mean 83.0% against 94.3%), so whatever reallocation the manager does is not something creation reveals. And the residual is the smallest signal in the study: `flow` scores a 63-day IC of +0.006 with a naive t of 1.3 - and the naive t is the inflated one. Conditioning on creation, redemption and quiet days leaves the share of positive outcomes at 0.52-0.56, the same as every other cut tried. NOTE ON CONSTRUCTION. Two corrections are applied here that the pack's other numbers do not carry. (1) Within one iShares document the par book and the shares_outstanding line are NOT the same session: pairing par(t)/par(t-1) with the share change reported on t+1 explains 99.9% of the gross par move, against -121.8% same-day and -18.7% the other way, so HP.flag_flow_days stamps the flow on the document AFTER the one holding the basket. (2) TLT's largest apparent creation, +37.8% on 2017-07-06, is a diff() across the six-month publication gap; every document pair more than 5 calendar days apart is dropped.
