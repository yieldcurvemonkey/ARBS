Who owns a US Treasury, over 430,140 unique bond-days, 2018-2026 (the window on which the aggregate-ownership panel is built). Every share on this figure uses the same denominator - the bond's TOTAL amount outstanding - so the three distributions can be read against each other; positions of exactly zero are excluded. On the study's own denominator, publicly held float (outstanding minus SOMA), the same medians are TLT 1.98% and the six funds combined 0.80%. The 1.72% quoted elsewhere in this pack is the same statistic over TLT's OWN 20+ universe back to 2016 rather than over this six-fund union panel from 2018; both are reproduced, and neither changes the conclusion. WHAT IT PROVES. Scale, and it is the number that settles the project. The Fed holds a median 17.8% of a Treasury issue and up to the 70% per-issue cap that shows as the hard right edge of its distribution. TLT holds a median 1.10%; all six scraped iShares funds together hold 0.61%. The two live nearly two orders of magnitude apart, and the small one is the one this study proposed to trade behind. WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE. That ownership is irrelevant - it is not. Aggregate ETF ownership co-moves with richness contemporaneously at +0.032bp per percentage point of float owned, and that survives a Newey-West correction (t 32 -> 8.8). What the scale does rule out is the mechanism the trade needed. A holder of ~1.5% of an issue cannot push it: regressing a bond's SAME-DAY richening on the fund's own realised trade in it, as a share of float, gives |t| <= 0.94 across all five funds tested (TLT -0.11, TLH -0.11, GOVT +0.39, IEF +0.78, IEI +0.94). That is a feasibility test rather than a signal - if a fund's trade cannot be found in the same day's price, no predictive version of it can work - and it fails. The contemporaneous ownership coefficient dies predictively too (multivariate 21-day t = 0.58), and SOMA's coefficient is NEGATIVE (-0.005bp/pp, HAC t -4.7), the wrong sign for scarcity, which marks the whole channel as a proxy for issue characteristics rather than a supply effect.
