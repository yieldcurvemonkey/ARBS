The original thesis: the fund has nothing left to buy

The eligible 20y+ board is 40 bonds, and TLT holds 68–95% of it (median 83%, rising to 95% by 2026). A manager already holding most of a 40-bond board is not choosing which bonds to buy. Raw active-weight IC is strong and backwards; controlling for the bond’s own richness flips every sign and collapses the edge to 0.003–0.005 bp/z.

A structural point that reframes the premise: at 20y+, a 0.25y bucket holds a median of one bond. At this end of the curve the “3-month risk bucket” is a per-CUSIP signal, not a bucket.

Intraday: the structure is real and an order of magnitude too small

Your intuition about the seam was right in direction. Opportunity does vary by hour and peaks where structure says it should — 09→10 at 0.1499 bp and the 15→16 seam at 0.1484 bp against a 13→14 trough of 0.1184 bp, a 1.27× peak-to-trough. It is simply too small: the seam is 0.188× of one round trip.

Real and worth keeping: a Treasury fly moves ~1.75× more per minute between 15:00 and 17:00 on the last business day of the month (permutation p = 0.0004), ramping to 2.30× in the quarter-hour before the NAV strike. It is a price effect, not activity — print counts are flat and the amplification grows on well-printed bonds.

Aggregation: it is TLT wearing a larger coat

On a paired test where only the fund set changed across 288 matched cells, aggregating cost −0.00072 bp/trade. Best partial-IC |t| was 2.48 for the aggregate against 2.52 for TLT alone. Every long-end fund holds 97–100% of the same small board and they choose nearly identically.

What would change the answer is not more funds — it is demand that is not an index tracker’s book: dealer inventory, primary-dealer positioning, or the STRIPS complex (EDV/ZROZ, 27 quarters already backfilled).

Open, and needing you

The nightly cron cannot run unattended — and that is the real blocker

Tonight jobs 9, 10 and 11 all failed with ExcelNotRunningError. The bridge attaches to a human-signed-in Excel and never spawns one, because spawned instances do not register the CV* UDFs. Three nights, three different causes: batch-0 abort, then a 12,501 MB memory guard, then no Excel at all. The branch fixes only the first.

It is solvable and today proved it: the intraday backfill agent launched Excel and signed in autonomously 3 of 3 times via the supervisor. The nightly does not use that path. Wiring it in is the missing piece.
