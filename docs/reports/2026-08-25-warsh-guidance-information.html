USD Rates · 25 Aug 2026 · post-audit

Talk, data, nothing

One thing about the Warsh regime is solidly measurable: the market is far less decided about the policy path than it was under guidance. Almost everything I first built on top of that did not survive an adversarial audit.

Correction

An earlier version of this page said the trader was wrong on both of his testable points. He wasn’t — and one of those tests couldn’t have answered him either way. Twelve independent audits were run against seven findings; eight came back refuted, including the headline. What follows is the version that survived.

The claim“Under Warsh, forward guidance is gone, so each speaker event carries more marginal information than under the old regime.”

The pushback“Pure optionality — he gets to decide what is real news and just stays silent when it suits him. No information is information!”

01 · The finding that held

Guidance ended on a specific Wednesday

How far the priced front meeting sits from an all-or-nothing outcome. 0 means the market has decided; 12.5bp — half a click — is as undecided as a single meeting gets. For the two weeks before 17 June it sat at 0.3–0.5. It has not been below 4.8 since.

Distance from a decided outcome, front FOMC meeting
Weekly mean, bp. Derived from the ZQ meeting ladder. Higher = less decided.
Guidance era Warsh, no guidance

Era means: 3.21bp under guidance against 8.29bp under Warsh. This is the one result that survived every attack — it holds under season-matching, after adjusting for how close the window sits to its next meeting, and against a placebo that slides a matched 49-session window through the whole guidance era: Warsh exceeds all 1,358 of them.

Two honest limits. The 49 Warsh sessions are only two distinct front meetings, against 44 on the other side. And a reading of 8.3 of a possible 12.5 is about a 1-in-3 chance of a move, not a coin toss — the priced move here is a hike.

02 · The finding that shrank

Talk, data, nothing

Sort every session into three kinds — Fed talk, a major US data release, and nothing — and ask what happened to that uncertainty over the following week. The raw picture is striking: under guidance talk was the best resolver on the board, and under Warsh it is the only one that resolves nothing. The raw picture is also mostly an artefact.

Guidance era Warsh era

Why this shrinks. Inside the Warsh window the five-day change is 53% explained by minus its own starting level (slope −1.03, t −6.8) — the measure mean-reverts hard. Event days happened to start 0.38bp lower than non-event days, which mechanically buys them a bigger rise. Control for the starting level and the event-vs-non-event contrast falls from +0.82 to +0.44bp, p 0.26. The guidance-era leg collapses too, to −0.11, p 0.31.

So the direction is real enough to keep looking at and nowhere near strong enough to trade. The claim I made earlier — that measuring inside one window made this immune to a period effect — is simply wrong: the same contrast is positive in 37–39% of arbitrary guidance-era windows.

03 · The finding that reversed

One statistic said what I wanted. The other didn’t.

The strongest point I had for the claim was that the events Warsh cannot decline lit up while the ones he chooses did not — the reverse of what a “he picks his moments” story predicts. That result exists only on the mean. On the median, both classes rise, and the asymmetry disappears.

The discretionary mean is the only one of the four that falls, and it is the one the earlier argument rested on. With six scheduled events and a handful of speeches, the mean is a hostage to one observation — and dropping the 14 Jul testimony, whose window contains a four-way dovish CPI miss 90 minutes earlier, takes the scheduled figure from 4.66 to 3.39, below both baselines.

04 · The test that couldn’t answer

I counted the committee. He was talking about one man.

“He stays silent when it suits him” is a claim about Warsh. The arrival-rate test counted every scored FOMC speaker — eleven of them in the window. It could not have detected Warsh going completely silent, because ten other people would have kept the count up.

Chair appearances in the 18 June – 25 August window

Warsh made 2 (JPM) or 3 (FedLock) appearances against Powell’s typical 3–4. That is directionally consistent with speaking a little less and nowhere near enough to establish it — the cell has 34–52% power against the deficit it observes. The committee-wide number I reported earlier is a fact about the committee, not about the chair.

05 · The better hypothesis · exploratory

“It’s a vote tallying exercise”

Jim Bianco’s reading, on MacroVoices the day after the July meeting: the chair used to whip the committee, so Fed-watching was chair-parsing. Warsh doesn’t, three members dissented in favour of a hike, and “Fed watching is not a game of parsing the chairman’s words… it’s a vote tallying exercise.” That predicts the chair carries less and every other voter carries more — and if both moved in opposite directions, pooling them into one “talk” bucket, which is what §2 did, would wash the effect to exactly the nothing it found.

His description of the old regime is confirmed, and it is the best-powered thing on this page after §1. Under guidance the chair moved the strip 3.03bp against the committee’s 1.87 and a quiet day’s 1.73, and resolved the most (−0.78 vs −0.52 vs −0.25). On 59, 387 and 959 days. The chair really was the event.

The prediction for the new regime does not show up. Committee speeches do not become better resolvers — they become the worst, at −0.12 against a quiet day’s −0.46. What did change is the chair’s price impact, from 3.03 to 5.11bp, while his resolution collapsed. Moves more, tells less — now concentrated in exactly the person Bianco says stopped answering.

And the dissenters go the other way. On the four names Bianco calls out, the path becomes less decided after they speak — +0.70 (JPM) and +1.00 (FedLock) against other members’ −0.95 and −0.42. Which may be the point rather than a contradiction: a dissenter reveals disagreement, and disagreement widens the distribution instead of narrowing it. That is information in one sense and the opposite of resolution in the other — the same wedge this whole page keeps running into.

Power. Two chair days in the Warsh window. Twelve committee days, six dissenter days. The guidance side is solid; the Warsh side is not a test, and this hypothesis arrived after the outcomes were seen, so it is not scored against the pre-registration.

Where it does earn its place: it supplies the mechanism for the one finding that held. If the chair no longer whips the committee, the distribution of outcomes genuinely widens — which is exactly the 3.21→8.29 in §1. The surviving result had no causal story until this one.

And it says something actionable that does not depend on any of the weak numbers: if it really is a vote-tallying exercise, the informative event is the meeting, where votes are revealed — not the speeches. That fits what this desk already measured elsewhere: the front-month risk premium survives to the day before and dies on the decision, +4.47bp.

The clean test of BoE-fication needs no speech corpus at all: count the dissents. Three at the July meeting is a hard countable fact, and dissent frequency under Warsh against Powell would settle the mechanism directly. This repo has no vote record — that is the gap worth closing next.

06 · Where it leaves the argument

What to say now

The two positions are still about different terms of the same thing. Information flow is the arrival rate of events times the information per event, plus everything arriving outside events. The claim is about information per event; the pushback is about the arrival rate and the outside channel. Both can be true at once — so even a clean finding on one would not have settled the other.

What can be said with confidence: the market is markedly less decided about the path than it was under guidance, and that is not a summer artefact, a proximity artefact, or a fluke of one window.

What cannot: whether Fed communication has stopped carrying information. The direction of every test points that way and not one of them clears its own null. The honest position is that the trader’s objection stands unrefuted — not that he is right, but that nothing here defeats him, and the test that looked like it did was measuring the wrong people.

For the book, that argues for patience rather than a position. The one tradeable consequence that follows from the surviving finding alone is about distribution, not direction: a path priced this far from a decided outcome is a path where the front meetings carry real two-way risk. Whether event volatility is mispriced against that is a separate question, and it needs the SR3 surface rather than the ladder.

Method. Uncertainty is measured as distance from an all-or-nothing outcome on the priced ZQ meeting ladder — |jump − 25×round(jump/25)| — because the Fed moves in 25bp clicks, so a meeting priced at a multiple of 25 is one the market has decided. Every window reads a fixed set of meetings chosen strictly after the window closes, so no measurement spans a meeting that resolved inside it. Both speech corpora are reported throughout.

What the audit changed. A prediction table was fixed before any outcome was computed. Twelve independent adversarial reviews were then run against seven findings, each told to refute rather than confirm; eight came back refuted and each counter-claim was recomputed here before being accepted. That process found a genuine code defect — silence was defined from the speech book, which does not contain the FOMC decision, so three of five “silence” gaps contained a set-piece — now fixed and covered by a test.

Limits. The Warsh era is 2.3 months: 23 events, six of them scheduled, six data days, and two distinct front meetings. Nothing here is a regime property yet. The scheduled-event cell becomes a real test around February 2027. Whether the regime lasts is not testable on 2.3 months and is not claimed.
