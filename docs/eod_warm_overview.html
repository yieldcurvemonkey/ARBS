Saturdays 10:00--backfill 7 — the past week, to catch anything a weekday lost
Loglogs/cache_warmer/, dated per run, 60 kept
Exit code0 all OK · 1 a job FAILED · 2 only SKIPPED

Before any job runs: the Excel pre-flight

Asked once, not rediscovered by every Velocity job. Twelve of the twenty-one depend on it.

It repairs rather than refuses

Four states, four remedies. No Excel → launch and wait for sign-in. Over the 3,800 MB ceiling → restart, rescuing unsaved work first. Signed out → press Login. Running but not bindable → restart, because a Login press cannot fix an Excel outside the Running Object Table.

A failed Login press escalates to a full kill and re-auth. Two remedies inside one latched repair — never a loop. force=True is never passed.

Memory is not liveness

The first three checks read a number from Get-Process. None of them asks whether the add-in answers, and an Excel at 500 MB with Velocity signed out passes all of them, then fails every Velocity job in turn. So the pre-flight ends with one bounded attach — the same call the jobs make thirty seconds later.

Measured cold start: 0 processes to signed-in and serving in 3.3 min, one Login press, against a 900 s budget.

SKIPPED is not FAILED

A job blocked by an unusable Excel wrote nothing, so its consumers still read the cumulative tag cache and run. A job that FAILED may have written half a partition, so its consumers are held back. That distinction is why the exit code means something.

Knobs: ARBS_WARM_EXCEL_AUTOSTART, ARBS_WARM_EXCEL_SIGNIN_TIMEOUT.

Nearly two million files, and that is the shape of the problem

The computed TS store holds 1,938,842 files for 9.0 GB — a mean under 5 KB. It is addressed per asset, and the Citi UST family alone contributes an asset=FRB__USTS_CITIVELO-RL__… directory per CUSIP per value. That is why an enumeration of it takes minutes, why a naive du over the cache root does not return, and why anything that walks the tree rather than reading the index is a mistake. The DuckDB mirror exists precisely so readers do not have to.

The computed TS path is repo-relative, and that is a trap

DEFAULT_COMPUTED_TS_BASE_DIR resolves to <checkout>/data/ts. The cron runs from the primary checkout, so that is where nightly values land — but a suite or script run from a worktree can resolve to the primary checkout anyway under full-suite import ordering, which makes it a production-write hazard from a place that looks isolated.

Most of AppData/Local/ARBS is a junction to D:\ARBS_DATA after the storage migration, so "local" cache paths are physically on D:.

What this does not cover

Stated so it is not rediscovered.

Only three jobs drive Excel

Jobs 10, 11 and 12 carry needs_excel. Jobs 8 and 9 shell out to scripts that drive Excel in their fetch phase but carry their own guards, and every job numbered 13 and above is deliberately offline — they read banked stores and refuse a live fallback, which is what stops a value job silently repricing off the wire.

Depth is bounded by memory, not time

The MI01 backward depth pass stops when Excel reaches its ceiling. Measured on the first production run: 1,664 bond-weeks, 1.15M rows, stopped at 64% of the time budget — about 0.96 MB of Excel per bond-week, so a year of depth for the 403 alive bonds is roughly 13 runs.

Known ragged edges

97 far-dated swaption expiries are unbuildable (no complete expiry x tenor rectangle survives the offset axis). The iShares holdings gap over 2017-01-03..2017-07-05 is the vendor's, re-confirmed empty on 2026-08-21, and nothing this cron does will fill it.
