## 2. Contract specifications and tick economics (known-answer facts)

Eurodollar (CME, Table 1.1 / A.2): notional $1,000,000; delivery months Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec + 4 serials = **40** delivery months; price = 100 − rate; min move **0.005** (0.0025 on front month); tick value **$12.50 per half bp** ($6.25 quarter-tick front month); last trading day = 2nd business day before 3rd Wednesday; margins (Nov/Dec 2011): initial $608–$1,013, maintenance $450–$750 (~75% of initial). Tick derivation: $1,000,000 × 0.005/100 × 0.25 = $12.50. **BPV = $25/bp** (Euribor €25, Euroswiss CHF25, Short Sterling £12.50 on £500,000 notional, 0.01 tick).

Other contracts (Table A.2): Euribor — Liffe, €1M, quarterly + 4 serials = 24 delivery months, min move 0.005 (€12.50); Short Sterling — Liffe, £500k, 22 delivery months, min 0.01 (£12.50); Euroswiss — Liffe, SFr1M, 8 quarterly months, min 0.01 (SFr25).

EDSP = 100 − 3M LIBOR fixing at 11:00 London on last trading day; cash-settled T+2.

Color banding: whites = quarterly ranks 1–4, then reds, greens, blues, golds; CME extends orange/pink/silver/copper (to 10 y). "Liffe Euribor stops at gold and Sterling at blue. Generally, liquidity can become poor after the blue packs."

## 3. Spread / butterfly / condor construction conventions (futures space)

- **Calendar spread:** P1 − P2, **nearest month always quoted first**; buy spread = buy near/sell far. Spread bid = near bid − far offer; spread offer = near offer − far bid (implied-in bounds); market quotes are tighter — typically the minimum increment (0.005 in Euribor).
- **Butterfly:** difference of two sequential spreads: (P1−P2) − (P2−P3) = **P1 + P3 − 2·P2**. Buy fly = buy nearest leg. Wings must be equidistant from body. Priced off outrights the fly bid/offer is 4 half-ticks wide (sum of 4 leg bid/offers); priced as S1 − S2 off the two calendar spreads it collapses to 1 tick, and market participants usually improve to the minimum increment. Worked example off the matrix: M2U2Z2 bid/offer from outrights −0.015 / +0.005; from spreads −0.005 / +0.005. "The price level of 0.000 is likely to be the aggregate of the last traded prices of the component futures. This is where the butterfly should trade" — deviations = RV opportunity vs outrights or component spreads. Most popular flies: sequential 3-month and sequential 1-year. Flies attract less margin than component spreads and are still directional (curvature around the body).
- **Condor:** **the SUM of two butterflies**, not the difference: (P1−P2) − (P3−P4); equivalently B1 + B2 or S1 − S3 (e.g. H2M2U2Z2 = H2M2 − U2Z2). Worked numbers: bid 0.040, offer 0.060; via flies 0.050 + 0.000 = 0.050; via spreads 0.105 − 0.055 = 0.050. Condors are MORE volatile than the fly or calendar (opposite views on two curve segments); multiple fees usually argue for a calendar instead.
- **Spread-from-spreads algebra:** 12-month spread = sum of the four 3-month spreads inside it = two 6-months = 9m+3m etc. Matrix "columns" and "rows" methods to convert an 18m−15m into a 3m spread, flip a long M2U2 into short U2Z2 via selling the 6m M2Z2, etc. Worked matrix trade: bid H2U2 0.155 (426 lots), sell H2M2 0.105 → long M2U2 at 0.050 on the bid; sell M2M3 0.195 vs buy U2M3 0.140 → sell M2U2 at 0.055; half-tick profit but 8 lots of fees vs 4 for the strategy. "Many 'matrix traders' live off the exchange rebates."
- **Volatility ordering (mean-reversion relevance):** outright Euribor stdev ≈ **0.23** vs 3-month calendar spread stdev ≈ **0.03**. Longer-interval spreads more volatile than shorter; **white-pack spreads ≈ 2× as volatile as red-pack spreads**, red > green (most curvature/expectation is in the first 8 quarterlies). Steepening widens calendars, flattening narrows them. Spread margin example: single Euribor initial €850; Tier-1 inter-month spread charge €250 (~25–30% of outright — SPAN treats a spread as ≈25% of outright risk); inter-contract credit example: Euribor €850 + 2y Swapnote €600 with 65% credit per leg.

## 4. Strips, packs, bundles, stacks

- **Strip:** any sequential run of contracts; generic, not exchange-traded except as packs/bundles.
- **Pack:** simultaneous equally-weighted purchase/sale of 4 quarterly contracts within a colour band (white pack = P1+P2+P3+P4, red = P5..P8, etc.).
- **Bundle:** all contracts from rank 1 out to N years (2y bundle = first 8 quarterlies = white+red; 3y = 12 = W+R+G; 4y = 16; 5y = 20 = W+R+G+B+Gold; CME lists to 10y, Liffe to 5y).
- **Stack:** multiple of a single month used as a pack/bundle proxy (4:1), accepting curve risk.
- **Quoting conventions:** packs/bundles priced on **net change from previous settlement**. Liffe = *total change convention* (sum of the four net changes, 0.005 increments); CME = *annualised convention* (simple average of net changes, **0.0025 increments**). Worked example: legs −0.03/−0.03/−0.03/−0.02 → Liffe −0.110, CME −0.0275. Half-tick averaging problem: legs averaging −0.02875 is not a valid CME price; trades print at −0.0275 or −0.0300 and the CME allocation algorithm assigns integer net changes adjusting the **most-deferred legs first**; Liffe's algorithm assigns generated prices keeping near/liquid legs closest to market. Liffe totals-quote appears more volatile (4 half-tick leg moves = 2-tick pack move vs 0.5 on CME).
- **Pack yield:** compound the four implied forward rates: [(1+r1·0.25)(1+r2·0.25)(1+r3·0.25)(1+r4·0.25)] − 1. Worked: prices 97.490/97.310/97.210/97.160 (rates 2.51/2.69/2.79/2.84) → **2.735%**. Forward-starting at first expiry; each quarter standardized 0.25y.
- **Bundle yield:** same product annualized to the power 1/N years. Worked 2y example adds H3..Z3 = 97.100/97.000/96.900/96.870 (2.90/3.00/3.10/3.13) → **2.901%** (power 0.5).
- **Intra-contract pack/bundle trades:** (1) bundle vs sum-of-packs arbitrage — "the tendency is for the bundles to be priced tighter than the sum of the packs"; fee-intensive, best on single-fee strategy pricing. (2) pack/bundle vs stack (4:1) — e.g. buy 4×M3 vs sell red pack ⇒ net = short H3M3U3 fly + long a 6m spread; pure price-improvement trade.

## 5. Convexity and the convexity bias (core for CA)

Pages 35–37 ("Advanced pricing concepts"):

- STIR futures have **fixed BPV ($25/€25) at any rate level — linear, no convexity**; bonds/swaps are convex (BPV falls as rates rise). Receiving fixed on a swap hedged by short STIR futures wins both ways from the gamma difference, plus the **daily-margin financing asymmetry**: rates up → futures profit credited and reinvested at higher rates; rates down → futures loss financed at lower rates.
- Consequence: "The advantages in being short STIR futures against receiving fixed swaps result in the markets quoting implied forward rates from STIR futures at higher rates (making the STIR futures price lower) than they would otherwise be. This effect is termed the **convexity bias**."
- "Convexity bias generally **increases with term** and is **driven by volatility and mean reversion of interest rates**." Reference cited: 'Convexity Conundrums', *Risk* (March 1997) [Burghardt et al.], www.powerfinance.com/convexity.
- **Known-answer number:** in the 1 Nov 2011 Euribor pricing example (Table 1.10), the convexity bias for **Euribor U4 (Sep-2014, ~2.9y out) = 4.6 bp**. U4 traded 97.905 ⇒ raw implied forward 2.095%, convexity-adjusted **2.045%** (book states "reduced to 2.045%"; note 2.095 − 0.046 = 2.049 — the book prints 2.045, a 0.4bp rounding discrepancy worth remembering as a tie-out caveat).

- **Value basis** definition: equivalent-term LIBOR/EURIBOR-derived forward rate − STIR-futures implied forward rate. Example: theoretical fwd 4.87% vs future 95.15 (implied 4.85%) → value basis 0.02% (futures "expensive in price / cheap in rates"). - **Stringing/chaining (matched-swap comparison):** compare a zero rate from deposits+swaps to a compounded futures strip zero. Worked: 1y swap DF 0.975 → zero (1/0.975)−1 = **2.564%**; futures strip Z1 98.00/H2 97.90/M2 97.80/U2 97.70 (2.0/2.1/2.2/2.3%) compounded → **2.167%** — futures strip "cheap in rate terms". On 1 Nov 2011 data the swap-vs-futures string basis ≈ **0.25% at the 2y point**, attributed to **term risk (tenor basis)**: 3s6s EURIBOR basis swaps traded ≈ 0.25% in Nov 2011, exactly closing the apparent arbitrage. (Direct analogue: SOFR CA has no tenor basis, so the residual is convexity + credit/term structure — this is the book's cleanest statement that the strip-vs-swap gap decomposes into convexity bias + tenor/term premium.)

## 6. Hedge ratios and matched-swap conventions

- Futures BPV: 1,000,000 × 0.0001 × 90/360 = €25. Bond BPV = dirty price × modified duration × 0.01%. Worked: Schatz CTD 0.25% 13-Dec-2013 — clean 100.13, accrued 0.037, MDur 1.92 → BPV/€100 = 0.0192; €10M position → 77 Euribor futures. Bond-future BPV = BPV(CTD)/conversion factor × contract size: 100,000 × (0.0191/100/0.906868) = **€21.02**; ratio vs €25 → 0.84 (84 Euribor per 100 Schatz).
- **Regression alternative:** hedge ratio = Beta (Excel SLOPE), validated by R² (RSQ); explicit warning that Beta/R² are sample-dependent. (This is the book's only "regression fair-value" content — methodology, no published coefficients.)
- **Tailing:** variation-margin leverage factor 1/(1 + i·(Dh/2 + Db)/B); worked: i=3%, Dh=60, Db=62 → **0.9924** (18.08 → 17.94 → round to 18 contracts). Beta example 1.05 (2M CP vs 3M EURIBOR).
- **Term TED spread** (2y credit spread, government bond vs futures strip — structural template for matched-maturity CA): build a notional bond from futures-strip discount factors matched to the bond's cash-flow dates (stub via interpolated 3M EURIBOR, then each quarterly IFR chained). Worked, €100M Schatz 0.25% 13-Dec-2013 on 20-Dec-2011 (settle 23-Dec): stub 23-Dec-11→21-Mar-12 (89 days) at interpolated 3M EURIBOR 1.406%, then futures H2 98.870, M2 98.985, U2 99.030, Z2 99.015, H3 98.985, M3 98.920, U3 98.855; adjusted bond price 98.362, accrued 0.0240, clean 98.3381, **strip yield 1.1063% vs cash bond yield 0.2118% → 2y term TED = 0.8945%**. Per-period futures hedge = (dirty price × 1bp × days/360)/€25 ≈ 99 per quarter, stub folded into first quarter (198 H2), **total 785 Euribor** vs €100M. Bloomberg TED function replicates.
- **Derivatives-only TED:** bond future vs 2y bundle. 11 Jan 2012: Euribor strip Mar-12 98.985, Jun-12 99.095, Sep-12 99.135, Dec-12 99.135, Mar-13 99.135, Jun-13 99.11, Sep-13 99.065, Dec-13 98.985 → strip yield 0.92%; Schatz H2 110.405 × CF 0.906868 = 100.123 → implied CTD yield 0.18% ⇒ **futures TED = 0.74%** (vs 0.895% cash version — forward-starting, maturity-mismatched ~3m, includes both bases). ## 7. Costs, microstructure, open interest

- **Costs:** round trip on one Euribor ≈ **€0.75–€1.50** vs €12.50 minimum-move value; example commission €2/round-trip in the macro trade (10 lots, 7 ticks: gross €1,750, fees €20). Liffe exchange fee £0.25/lot + clearing £0.03/lot; clearing-member commission £0.03–£0.25/lot. Liffe LP scheme saves £0.10/lot in reds, £0.15 greens, £0.20 blues; back-month all-in cost cut by roughly **one-third to one-half**. CME IIP: unregistered ED fee $1.19/lot → $0.44. Fixed costs ~£1,000/month (software £250–£1,250/mo etc.). Repeated warnings that 4-leg flies and 8-leg matrix trades are fee-dominated; "many matrix traders live off the exchange rebates."
- **Implied pricing:** exchange-supported implied-in (outrights→strategies) and implied-out (strategies→outrights); execution guaranteed. Liffe supports implied-out only for calendar spreads; **CME extends implied-out to butterflies**. Implied-upon-implied is ISV-side only, no execution guarantee. Real snapshot (Liffe Short Sterling 10 Feb 2006 10:01, Table 2.4): H6 21,996 bid of which only 887 implied; H6M6 spread offer 13,969 of which 11,869 implied off the M6 bid — "not as liquid as it first appeared"; fly M6U6Z6 would be −0.06 bid by implication with an actual −0.05 trader bid. Trading rules: lean **against** implieds (bid when the offer is implied); implied prices vanish when the parent order goes; strategies (Liffe flies/condors/packs/bundles, no implied support) can trade outside implied bounds in fast markets = opportunity (Ten Rules #5).
- **Order allocation:** both exchanges pro-rata (with top-order and partial-FIFO variants); 3M spreads can show 10,000+ lots per side vs ~1,000 in outrights, so join-the-queue fills are poor — hence matrix/auto-spreader entry. Auto-spreaders leg with execution risk; configure size multiples (e.g. 2×) and ignore implied-only backing.
- **Open interest / liquidity (Table 1.12, Eurodollar close 8 Dec 2011 — settle/change/volume/OI):** Dec-11 99.4475 / +0.005 / 164,537 / 973,289; Jan-12 99.435 / 4,096 / 28,177; Feb-12 99.420 / 62 / 6,557; Mar-12 99.390 / 241,149 / 906,333; Apr-12 99.390 / 0 / 426; May-12 99.375 / 0 / 255; Jun-12 99.360 / 256,590 / 1,092,429; Sep-12 99.350 / 185,540 / 779,396; Dec-12 99.350 / 171,105 / 758,081; Mar-13 99.360 / 171,582 / 770,612; Jun-13 99.345 / 146,899 / 569,078; Sep-13 99.315 / 109,552 / 445,338; Dec-13 99.240 / 111,480 / 521,817; Mar-14 99.130 / 87,211 / 365,495; Jun-14 98.975 / 74,571 / 304,308; Sep-14 98.805 / 71,170 / 214,183. Interpretation: liquidity peaks in ranks 2–3 (H2/M2), not the near-expiry front; only the first serial has volume; volume declines steadily but tangible OI persists to ~2018 contracts. December contracts trade at a slight yield premium (year-end turn / Y2K memory). Liquidity preference concentrates in whites; micro-curve kinks from large single-month trades are the intraday RV pond.
- **HFT/algos (2012 state):** up to 60% of volume ascribed to algo/HFT; four premise classes — technical, **statistical arbitrage ("mean reversion … price history is used to calculate trading bands around a mean, and these are used as the base for trade initiation")**, event, predatory. Liquidity transitory; economic releases untradeable for humans; co-location on Liffe Connect with single-digit-ms round trips.

## 8. Signal/entry-exit content (qualitative — no fitted equations)

The book publishes **no regression fair-value model with coefficients** and no CA-vs-fly signal. Signal-relevant content: (a) mean-reversion framing for spreads/flies under statistical arbitrage (bands around a historic mean); (b) fly fair value = aggregate of last-traded component prices — trade deviations against outrights/spreads; (c) bundle tighter than sum-of-packs tendency; (d) TED/swap-spread divergence trades (worked examples §6 with entry on divergence, exit on convergence, 1–5 day holds); (e) technical-indicator survey (SMA crossovers, ADX>30 trending / <20 trendless, RSI 70/30, Bollinger 20d/2σ, ROC, Gann, Fibonacci 38.2/50/61.8) with the author's verdict that they look better in hindsight (Ten Rules #9); (f) "variant perception" — trade the gap between consensus and futures-implied pricing for asymmetric payoff. Ten Rules for Trading STIR Futures (p.245) list riskordering: inter-contract > intra-contract; wider intervals > narrower; strategies priced tighter than implied.
